Hurricane Evacuation Zones - Complete Guide

Hurricane evacuation zones are designated areas that may need to be evacuated based on storm surge risk, wind speed, and hurricane category. Zones are typically labeled A through E, with Zone A being the highest risk.

Zone A - HIGHEST RISK (Evacuate First)
- Location: Coastal areas, barrier islands, immediate coastline
- Risk: Direct ocean exposure, highest storm surge risk
- Elevation: Typically 0-10 feet above sea level
- When to evacuate: Category 1+ hurricanes
- Storm surge potential: 4-20+ feet depending on storm
- Examples: Beachfront properties, low-lying coastal communities, areas directly on bays and sounds

Mandatory evacuation: ALL Category 3+ hurricanes
Recommended evacuation: Category 1-2 hurricanes

Zone B - HIGH RISK
- Location: Near coastal areas, low-lying inland areas prone to surge
- Risk: Significant storm surge risk, flooding
- Elevation: Typically 10-20 feet above sea level
- When to evacuate: Category 2+ hurricanes
- Storm surge potential: 6-15 feet
- Examples: Areas within 1-2 miles of coast, flood-prone neighborhoods

Mandatory evacuation: Category 3+ hurricanes
Recommended evacuation: Category 2 hurricanes

Zone C - MODERATE RISK
- Location: Inland areas with some elevation, older mobile homes
- Risk: Moderate storm surge and wind damage
- Elevation: Typically 20-30 feet above sea level
- When to evacuate: Category 3+ hurricanes
- Storm surge potential: 9-12 feet in worst scenarios
- Examples: Areas 2-5 miles from coast, low-lying inland regions

Mandatory evacuation: Category 4+ hurricanes
Recommended evacuation: Category 3 hurricanes

Zone D - LOWER RISK
- Location: Further inland, higher elevation areas
- Risk: Wind damage, power outages, falling trees
- Elevation: Typically 30+ feet above sea level
- When to evacuate: Category 4+ hurricanes (selective)
- Storm surge potential: Minimal
- Examples: Inland communities, areas 5+ miles from coast

Evacuation: Only for mobile homes or weak structures in Category 4+

Zone E - LOWEST RISK
- Location: Well inland, highest elevation
- Risk: Primarily wind damage and power outages
- Elevation: Typically 40+ feet above sea level
- When to evacuate: Rare, only extreme Category 5 with catastrophic winds
- Storm surge potential: None
- Examples: Inland cities, areas 10+ miles from coast

Evacuation: Rarely required, but shelter-in-place with preparations

Special Evacuation Categories

Mobile Homes and Manufactured Housing
- Evacuate for ALL hurricanes (Category 1+)
- Not designed for hurricane-force winds
- Risk of complete destruction in Category 2+
- Move to designated shelters or inland with friends/family

High-Rise Buildings (10+ floors)
- Evacuate upper floors (10+) for Category 3+ hurricanes
- Risk of extreme wind speeds at height
- Elevator failure risk
- Move to lower floors or evacuate building entirely

Medical Facilities and Nursing Homes
- Special evacuation plans required
- Often evacuate earlier (24-48 hours before landfall)
- Coordinate with local emergency management
- May require medical transport assistance

Evacuation Timing by Category

Category 1 Hurricane (74-95 mph):
- Zone A: Evacuate 12-24 hours before landfall
- Mobile homes: Evacuate 12-24 hours before landfall
- Others: Shelter in place with preparations

Category 2 Hurricane (96-110 mph):
- Zone A: Evacuate 18-36 hours before landfall
- Zone B: Evacuate 12-24 hours before landfall
- Mobile homes: Evacuate 18-36 hours before landfall
- Others: Prepare for potential evacuation

Category 3 Hurricane (111-129 mph) - MAJOR:
- Zone A: Evacuate 24-48 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- Zone B: Evacuate 18-36 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- Zone C: Evacuate 12-24 hours before landfall (RECOMMENDED)
- Mobile homes: Evacuate 24-48 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- High-rises: Evacuate upper floors

Category 4 Hurricane (130-156 mph) - MAJOR:
- Zone A: Evacuate 36-48 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- Zone B: Evacuate 24-36 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- Zone C: Evacuate 18-24 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- Zone D: Evacuate 12-18 hours before landfall (RECOMMENDED)
- Mobile homes: Evacuate 36-48 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- High-rises: Evacuate building or move to lower floors

Category 5 Hurricane (157+ mph) - CATASTROPHIC:
- ALL ZONES A-D: Evacuate 48-72 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- Zone E: Evaluate evacuation 24 hours before landfall
- Mobile homes: Evacuate 48-72 hours before landfall (MANDATORY)
- High-rises: Evacuate building entirely
- Large-scale evacuations expected

How to Know Your Evacuation Zone

1. Check local emergency management website
2. Call local emergency management office: 311 or county emergency number
3. Use online evacuation zone lookup tools (county-specific)
4. Look for evacuation zone signs in your neighborhood
5. Check your property deed or homeowner's insurance documents

Example Zone Lookup Resources:
- Florida: https://www.floridadisaster.org/knowyourzone/
- Texas: https://www.tdem.texas.gov/
- Louisiana: https://www.getagameplan.org/
- North Carolina: https://readync.org/
- South Carolina: https://www.scemd.org/

Evacuation Routes

- Pre-designated routes marked with blue "EVACUATION ROUTE" signs
- Avoid coastal routes during evacuation
- Move inland and away from storm path
- Fill gas tank early (stations may run out or lose power)
- Expect heavy traffic, allow extra time
- Do NOT wait until mandatory evacuation to leave

Evacuation Shelters

Types of Shelters:
1. Public shelters (schools, community centers)
2. Pet-friendly shelters (limited availability)
3. Special needs shelters (medical equipment, mobility issues)
4. Hotels/motels inland
5. Friends/family out of evacuation zones

Shelter Guidelines:
- Bring identification, medications, important documents
- Bring bedding, pillows, comfort items
- Bring food and water (1-2 days supply)
- Shelters provide basic safety, not comfort
- Register early for special needs shelters

What NOT to Do During Evacuation

❌ Do NOT wait for mandatory evacuation to leave (leave early!)
❌ Do NOT evacuate to another coastal area
❌ Do NOT drive through flooded roads ("Turn Around, Don't Drown")
❌ Do NOT return until officials say it's safe
❌ Do NOT assume you can ride out Category 3+ in your home
❌ Do NOT forget pets (bring them or arrange care)

Critical Reminders

✅ Evacuate early, before mandatory orders
✅ Know your zone BEFORE hurricane season
✅ Have evacuation plan for family and pets
✅ Keep important documents in waterproof container
✅ Fill gas tank when storm is 3-5 days away
✅ Bring medications and medical equipment
✅ Tell someone outside the area your evacuation plan
✅ Follow local emergency management on social media
✅ Monitor NOAA Weather Radio and local news

Remember: Storm surge kills more people than wind. If told to evacuate, EVACUATE. Your home can be rebuilt, but you cannot be replaced.
